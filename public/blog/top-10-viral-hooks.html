Short-Form Strategy

10 Hook Patterns for Short-Form Videos

There is no single formula for a successful video opening. These patterns are useful starting points for making the first few seconds specific and easy to understand.

A practical creator reference
01

The unexpected result

Lead with a surprising outcome, then explain what caused it.

02

The common mistake

Name a mistake your audience is likely making and promise a clearer approach.

03

The specific question

Ask a question that describes a real problem rather than a broad topic.

04

The quick demonstration

Show the result or transformation first and explain the steps afterward.

05

The strong opinion

State a defensible point of view that gives the viewer a reason to hear your argument.

06

The before-and-after

Contrast an earlier state with a better result and reveal how the change happened.

07

The short story

Start at the most interesting moment of a real story instead of beginning with background.

08

The useful shortcut

Offer a concise way to save time, avoid repetition or simplify a familiar task.

09

The misconception

Correct a widely repeated assumption and explain the more useful way to think about it.

10

The open loop

Introduce an unanswered question or incomplete result that the rest of the clip resolves.

Find hooks inside long videos

Instead of brainstorming from scratch, search your existing transcripts for questions, strong claims, surprising statements and story moments. Then turn the strongest sections into focused clips.
